## Case Study 2: Liverpool FC (2024/25)
<img width="320" height="340" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/89ddea1e-d850-416a-9303-d1200a6ef0d3" />

In 2024/25 Liverpool FC placed 1st, lifting the Premier League trophy, whilst also ranking 1st for Value Efficiency (both gold).

**Context and Interpretation:**

- Liverpool ranked 1st across all three seasons (2023/24-2025/26) for Value Efficiency, generating 20 points per €10m spent, considerably outperforming every other club in the dataset.
- At the end of the previous season, Liverpool's manager of more than eight years departed the club as one of the most successful Premier League managers of all time, with Arne Slot beginning a new managerial era.
- There have been several examples of clubs struggling to maintain success after replacing long-serving managers, such as Manchester United after Sir Alex Ferguson and Arsenal after Arsène Wenger.
- Despite entering a new managerial era, Liverpool invested relatively little in the transfer market compared with many of their direct competitors.
- This may suggest the club placed greater emphasis on maximising the performance of an already strong squad rather than undertaking a major squad rebuild during the new manager's first season.

**KEY INSIGHT**: 

Liverpool’s 2024/25 league campaign stands out as an outlier within the dataset. The club won the Premier League title while investing relatively little into the transfer market, and recorded highest Transfer Value Efficiency across all three seasons analysed, outperforming the second-highest club by 7.2 points per €10m.

This suggests that success isn’t solely dependent on significant transfer spending. In Liverpool’s case, creating value by maximising the current squad of players rather than investing heavily in transfers, proved to be just as important means to achieving Premier League success.

## Case Study 3: Chelsea (2023/24)

In 2023/24 Chelsea placed 6th, securing a place in a European competition (orange), but ranked last/20th for Value Efficiency (red).

**Context and Interpretation:**

- Chelsea demonstrated poor transfer efficiency, recording 1.41 Points per €10m spent. Chelsea’s Total Spend (€448.1m) this season is the 2nd greatest in the dataset, slightly behind Liverpool’s 25/26 season, who won the league the season before.
- At the start of the previous season, new owners took over after purchasing the club. Their new transfer ideology led the club to invest a significant amount in the transfer window, which continued into the 2023/24 season.
- Given the scale of Chelsea's transfer investment, a significantly stronger league performance would usually be expected, aiming for a Champions League spot. However, despite recording one of the highest transfer expenditures in the dataset, the club finished sixth and produced the lowest Transfer Value Efficiency of the 2023/24 season.

**KEY INSIGHT**: 

Despite Chelsea heavily investing into transfers during the 2023/24 season, the club was not efficient, only garnering 1.41 Points per €10m spent. 

Greater spending alone does not guarantee efficient performance outcomes. This suggests that investment quantity isn’t necessarily better than quality in terms of success in the Premier League, but that it’s also important to effectively recruit to build a coherent squad under a trusted manager/coach.
